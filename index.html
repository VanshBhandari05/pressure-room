Definition

What is pressure?

A ball is a pressure ball when what happens on it can swing the result a lot. For every ball we ask the model how likely each outcome is (dot, one, two, three, four, six, a wicket, and three kinds of wide or no-ball), work out the win probability after each, and take the expected swing. Divide by the average swing over live balls and you have the pressure score, called leverage.

Runs needed and balls left

The required rate and the stage of the innings decide how far one boundary or one wicket moves the chase.

Wickets in hand

With few wickets left, one dismissal can end the chase; with many, the same ball hardly matters.

Which innings

The model works in the first innings as well, in the same unit as the chase, so the two are comparable.

The shape of a match

Where pressure lives

Pressure is not spread evenly. It gathers where the result is still open and the margin for error is thin.

Proof

Pressure changes the game

If our definition picks out real pressure, play should look different on those balls. It does: batters attack more, bowlers reach for yorkers and slower balls, and the outcomes move. Blue always means normal conditions, pink always means pressure.

Method

How a player is rated

Three steps: a baseline, a pressure performance, and an adjusted rating. Every ball is valued by the win probability it added, split between batter and bowler, and one regression per role compares each player with the opposition they actually faced.
